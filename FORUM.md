## Ward Beck pre's

_WBS INFO · May 26, 2007|40_

> Ward Beck had a design that worked and put the same thing in an entire series so the pre-amp is almost identical in all of the L series it's how the signal goes out of the module that is different. It really depends on here you tap the output. 
>
> The M470's are the most flexible in terms of routing, so if you're looking for a console with flexibility fit them with M470's. within the M470's their are several variations different aux buses, pan pots master outputs. The big one and most flexible is the M470C it has 4 Aux buses, 6 sub group sends and pan pot. since some consoles were used as mono only, some M470's dont even have a pan pot. All M470's are swappable, so if you have a console with them any variation will pop in without any problems. I keep a set of M470C's kicking around so when a M470A module in my console dies or needs service they are standing by. 
>
> The M490's are identical pre-amps as th M470's just smaller with a fader fitted in to the module. they were used in radio broadcast consoles. Limited routing and they have a very flexible remote button section. 
> The M480 is a great module it combines a bit of the L series pre-amp and the EQ of say a scaled back M472 (the M480 has fixed LF and HF points) the M480's were only fitted in the T1202 transportable mixer. 
>
> The M460's use a different transformer and a different booster amp setup various eq and routing options are availible on these modules, they make great rack mounted pieces
> The M461 is identical input section as M460's but has no EQ
> The M441's are the most sought after and the closest to the original neve design that was copied. Best transformers, detended pots very little circuit boards all hand made. great pieces! 
> The M600 is the same vintage as the M441's they were designed to be remote pre-amps or used in intercom racks. they share some components as the M441's but are a rack tray module with only a gain pot not a console module
>
> i hope this helps, and if I'm wrong on some details sorry in advance

> Hi Guys, the M461 is basically a M460 without the EQ section. I have a manual for one in my shop but will not be there until Sunday. Like all Ward Beck M46xxxx series modules the Pin 22 is chassis, Pin 21 is +25 volts, Pin 20 is common, pin 19 is -25 volts. Pin 18 should be strapped to the common (p20) to have the LED's light. Pin 21 must be strapped to Y and Z to W in order to get the channel on light to come on. I usually put an 82 ohm resistor between Z and W and pin 20 should be strapped to pin X. The resistor drops the 25 volts down to about 23 volts and the incandescent lamp will last much longer.
>
> The balanced inserts pins must be strapped together and the balanced faders pins must be strapped together before any audio will get through to the final output stage. 
>
> Once I find the manual I can give you better wiring notes.
>
> Best regards, Dave Thomas www.aamicrophones.com

## m 460 termination?

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Jun 25, 2010|43_

> here is a hookup diagram I made for someone IGNORE that it's for an M461 not a 460
> take the pad network and apply it here 
>
> P1-19 --510ohm-- P1-20
>  |
>  |
>  2
>  2
>  0
>  |
>  |
> P1- X --510ohm-- P1-W
>
> it uses 510, 510 and 220 as the resistors making a 15 db pad. 
>
> as for the channel on thing... it's more of a preference thing. for me, when racking, if I can avoid using switches I will. So, by bypassing the ch on you eliminate a switch, but you also eliminate the phase flip switch... on later WBS this was on the input not on the insert return. 
>
> NEVER MIND
>
> just put this pad in... that will help
>
> The switches go bad, and the pots get crackely... this is normal. The pots are sealed and don't open or allow for cleaning... they're old this is ok just use it often and it wont go bad. if the switches are dirty, I've been using MG super contact cleaner... it works very well

## Help restoring my WBS 1204

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Feb 15, 2012|45_

> Hello Cassidy.
>
> I presume this is your first restoration effort? It is best to start with the power-supplies - rebuild them first and clean them up. Next would be to pull all EQ's and input modules - and listen to the sub and master sections. Then take (1) M461 module and walk it across each input - terminate its input with a line level signal like an oscillator from the console or protools, and do measurements to listen to the routing and busing. Next do the same with an EQ and the same M461. Once you have determined that the ins/outs/assignments are relatively OK - then deal with each module on a 1 by 1 basis to determine who is sick and who is OK. At this stage do NOT worry about cleaning switches, etc... just get the audio to pass cleanly. I believe this board sat for a few years - so pull all the summing amplifyer cards - the M450B's and put a little WD40 on the edge connectors and re-seat them (not a bad idea for every gold connection point in the console - just a bit on a cloth to rub on and off).
>
> Ideally you will be able to determine if the console works. Then who is sick, and of whom is sick what the illness is, etc... I forget - does this board have a built in M405 If not you should buy one. It is an extended range VU meter. You can accurately measure the signal going in and out of anywhere in the console thus determining where you drop -15 db etc.. where you are high (umm audio levels please) etc... 
>
> As a last point of interest - not only should you open up each module and re-seat each daughter board on its mother board, but if this console were mine - I would replace EVERY single LM201 to a modern LM301, and I would do the same on all op-amps in the EQ as well. You will experience a very significant drop in self noise and distortion - almost bring it back to factory spec.
>
> OH and get a copy of the manual - Theo had a similar board. Ask him to whom he sold it and bug them for a photocopy. The WBSPS will have a manual for a 1204 console posted soon, but I have to get to it first!!!
>
> Good luck.
>
> Wb!

— **warrenbeck**, Feb 15, 2012|45

> Hello.
>
> Well the LM201 to 301 will be a HUGE change. I advise putting the op-amps in sockets unless they are already in there. Do not use the silly machined sockets - use dual wiper gas-less seal AMP sockets. They will make service better for you in the long run.
>
> Exploring all the sick modules are good. I doubt the 4 electrolytics on the modules are bad. I would pull the daughter boards, test the base module for voltage - take the daughter boards form the sick modules and plug them one by one in to a healthy module to further isolate who is sick of the bunch. On the M461's could be a 4-110 board, on the EQ's could be an 8-109 (4-109 depending upon vintage), etc... this will help narrow down what is bad, and eliminate expensive tech time - as they charge by the hour!! Also by pulling the daughter boards gently crimp the pins with needle nose pliers to make them a little more compressive/stiff. THat will help in making a secure connection - as we do not know what the CBC or the TV station did over the years with this board. 
>
> Most likely they are a sick LM201 - they do die over time!! I had one that had cracked in half in one of my M402B's. Heat is an enemy!!!
>
> You are 70% there. This business above YOU should be able to do - and let the tech replace the op-amps.
>
> For the power-supplies have the tech do the m first. THe computer grade caps most likely will not need replacing - but Mouser, Digi Key, and Electrosonic all have the appropriate caps in stock and they still fit, etc...
>
> Good luck and let us know of your developments.

## M461M schematic

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Feb 15, 2013|46_

> My units just say M461. I ended looking at other schematics around these numbers and they all look somewhat similar. Is there a standard M461 schematic? I can't seem to find much info on it.

— **Santiago**, Feb 19, 2013|46

> The M461 is standard across all M461 modules - regardless of the suffix. The suffix typically denotes the option of a customer special routing. Largely they are all interchangeable, hot swappable, etc... just have to wire your console for the auxes/summing/RS/FB/PA etc...
>
> The ONLY exception to this is the M460M - it was designed after some whyning by the engineering department, and not by Dad. They did silly things to the switch, and routing. It can all be re-configured to be a normal M460 (straight through to and including LA for pin-outs not necessarily function). But after that headache all modules conformed to 1 consistent specification for its series.
